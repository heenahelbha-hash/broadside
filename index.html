WORDS STILL WORTH SETTING BY HAND

Broadside is a community print shop — three Vandercook presses, forty cases of wood and metal type, and open studio hours every week for anyone who wants ink on their hands.

Book a studio hour

We're a nonprofit shop above a bicycle repair store, kept alive by dues, workshop fees, and volunteers who show up on Tuesdays to distribute type back into its cases. If you've never touched a composing stick, that's the usual way people start here.

How a job gets made

The same three moves, whether it's a wedding invite or a protest poster.

  1. 01

    Compose

    Type is picked letter by letter from the case and locked into a composing stick, backwards and upside down, until the line reads true.

  2. 02

    Ink

    A thin, even film goes down with a brayer — too much and the letterforms fill in, too little and the impression starves.

  3. 03

    Pull

    Paper meets type under real pressure. Every sheet gets pulled by hand, one at a time, which is why no two runs are quite identical.
