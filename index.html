01 HOW A JOB GETS MADE • THE THREE DISCIPLINARY STAGES NO SHORTCUTS • FROM CASE TO DRYING RACK
STAGE 01

COMPOSE

From California Job Case to Locked Chase
PICA GAUGE 28
B R O A D S I D E ❦
HAND-SETTING LETTER BY LETTER UPSIDE DOWN & LEFT-TO-RIGHT

Every broadside begins at the type cabinet. Letters are picked by hand from the California job case into a composing stick, spaced out with brass and copper thins, and justified until tight.

The form is transferred to the stone imposition slab, surrounded by wood furniture and quoins, then locked tightly in a cast-iron chase with a quoin key. It must be "stone-lift"—if a single letter drops when lifted, the lockup is rejected.

Composing Stick California Case Line Gauge Quoins & Key
STAGE 02

INK

Marble Slab, Brayer Drawdown & Tack
LISTEN FOR THE HISS: UNIFORM TACK WITH ZERO ORANGE PEEL

Letterpress ink is stiff, oil-based, and rich with pigment. A walnut-sized dollop is worked across the ground marble slab using an ink knife until butter-smooth, then rolled out with a handheld composition brayer.

Printers listen intently for the "hiss"—a velvet whisper that indicates the ink film is perfectly distributed across the rollers. Too wet, and letter shoulders slur; too dry, and wood grain goes hungry.

Marble Slab Linseed Base Composition Brayer Palette Knife
STAGE 03

PULL

Tympan Packing, Cylinder Sweep & Bite
300g DEBOSS
↓ IMPRESSION BITE
SMOOTH 180° CRANK SWEEP UNDER FIVE HUNDRED POUNDS OF PACKING

The sheet of damp or dry cotton rag is registered against brass gauge pins upon the tympan packing. The printer turns the heavy trip lever and rolls the cylinder smoothly across the inked bed.

Steel kisses wood and lead; hundreds of pounds of cylinder pressure press the pigment deep into the cotton fibers, leaving that unmistakable dimensional letterpress bite. The fresh sheet is peeled, inspected for registration, and hung upon line racks to cure.
